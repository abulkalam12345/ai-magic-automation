# WonderVexa first proof: reference-frame specification

**Status:** Visual reference concept prepared; image file is not yet stored in this repository.  
**Story:** Wilted Flower Restoration.  
**Target:** 9:16 vertical, one flower, moonlit garden, restrained blue-and-gold magic.

## Canonical visual identity

Use these details consistently in every still or video prompt:

- Exactly one bluebell-like flower, one slender green stem, and one recognizable flower head.
- Blue-violet petals; the opening state is visibly wilted, folded, and drooping.
- A small patch of dark, moist garden soil at the bottom of frame.
- Deep blue moonlit garden background with soft, uncluttered bokeh.
- A thin, elegant ribbon of blue and warm-gold light; only a few small particles.
- Macro camera at flower height, shallow depth of field, stable vertical framing.
- No people, hands, extra flowers, text, logos, signatures, or baked-in watermark.

## Reference still prompt

Create an original cinematic fantasy macro still in vertical 9:16. Show exactly one delicate bluebell-like flower growing from a small patch of dark garden soil at night. Its single slender green stem is bent, and its blue-violet petals are folded, dry, and drooping so the wilted condition is unmistakable. A thin, restrained ribbon of luminous blue and warm-gold light curls gently around the flower, with only a few tiny glowing particles. A soft moon glows in the distant upper-right background; the garden behind it is deep blue and softly blurred. Keep the entire flower and most of its stem visible, centered slightly above the vertical midpoint. Cinematic high-detail fantasy realism, natural botanical structure, elegant lighting, shallow depth of field, calm magical atmosphere. No text, no logo, no watermark.

## Continuity rules for generated clips

1. Reuse the same reference image, aspect ratio, camera framing, and style settings for every clip whenever the provider supports image conditioning.
2. Keep the same flower species, flower-head position, stem curve, petal color, soil patch, moon direction, and background bokeh.
3. Clip A ends with the flower still wilted and the blue-gold glow gathering near the stem.
4. Clip B begins from that state and performs the restoration gradually; it must not introduce a different flower or location.
5. Reject clips with extra stems, duplicated petals, sudden camera changes, morph jumps, flicker, or an instant pop-open.
6. The still is a composition and identity reference, not proof that a video provider is free, licensed, or capable of temporal continuity.
